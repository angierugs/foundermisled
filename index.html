Same idea.
Built the opposite way.

Employer review sites aren't a new idea. They mostly fail the same way: the anonymity is a promise rather than a design, the score can't tell a good year from an old one, and nobody can see what gets removed. Here's every place we made the other call.

Reading what people wrote

Them

Post a review to unlock the reviews. A wall between you and the only thing you came for, which quietly guarantees a pile of throwaway posts written to get past it.

Us

Free to read, forever. No wall, no account, no email. You contribute because you want to, which is the only way the writing stays worth reading.

What anonymity actually means

Them

Anonymity is a policy. Your identity is collected, then promises are made about it, and policies change hands when companies do.

Us

Anonymity is architecture. No accounts, no email, no identity collected at any point. We can't hand over what we never had, and no future owner can decide otherwise.

Who you're allowed to name

Them

Either a blanket ban that stops you describing the person actually causing the problem, or no real limit at all.

Us

Officers only, in a labelled opinion. Founders, CEOs, and C-suite can be named. Coworkers never can, and an automated scan catches names and contact details before anything publishes.

Fact and opinion in one box

Them

One text field. "They promised a refresh in writing and never issued it" sits alongside "my boss is a monster," formatted identically, weighted identically.

Us

Two separate post types. Records state facts. Opinions get stamped as opinions and carry six disclosures, including how you left and whether you're in a dispute, printed next to every word.

How much old scores count

Them

A review from 2019 weighs exactly as much as one from this morning. Companies that genuinely fixed themselves stay buried; ones coasting on an old reputation stay propped up.

Us

Two-year half-life, plus a trend line. Recent ratings dominate the Founder Index, and the company page plots how the score moved so you can see direction, not just position.

Review drives after bad press

Them

A flood of fresh five-stars following a layoff round looks identical to genuine improvement. The average absorbs it silently.

Us

Surge flags, in public. If half a company's ratings landed inside one week, the page says so. We don't delete them or discount them, we show you the shape and let you judge.

What the number measures

Them

A single star average, built from categories generic enough to apply to a bank and a bakery. A 3.8 tells you nothing actionable.

Us

Five questions that only bite under a founder. Straight answers, decisions that hold, equity you can trust, room to disagree, life outside it, plus a "where it breaks" readout naming the weakest axis.

When a post disappears

Them

It's just gone. No notice, no reason, no count. Which is exactly why people assume employers can buy removals.

Us

A public transparency log. Every takedown notice, its outcome, and the written reason. A moderator can't decide anything without publishing why.

What money can buy

Them

Placement, branding, and a widespread belief, fair or not, that spending moves the number.

Us

Listings and a right of reply. Never the score. Job posting is free for everyone. The Founder Index is computed from employee ratings alone, and no tier removes, reorders, or suppresses a post.

Employer responses

Them

A paragraph of PR boilerplate thanking you for your feedback and inviting you to reach out to HR.

Us

A structured reply. Three fields: what we're changing, what we dispute, what we won't change. A founder who won't fill those in has told you something too.

Deleting your own post

Them

Requires signing into the account you never wanted to make. Change your mind after clearing your browser and you're stuck.

Us

A one-time deletion key. Issued the moment you post, works from any device, no account. We store only a one-way hash, so nobody here can look it up, or be pressured into it.

Somewhere to just be angry

Them

Nowhere. Every word is permanent, public, and attached to a company forever.

Us

A vent room that empties itself. Twenty-four hours, no company attached, no effect on any score. Some days you don't want to file anything.

Where the incumbents genuinely beat us

Worth saying plainly, because a comparison page that claims to win every row isn't worth reading.

  • Coverage. They hold millions of reviews across every industry on earth. We cover founder-led companies and nothing else. If you're weighing an offer from a bank, they're more use than we are.
  • Salary data. Theirs is deep, ours doesn't exist yet. Compensation ranges here come only from what a job poster chose to write down.
  • Volume beats method. A blunt average over four hundred reviews can be more reliable than a careful index over six. Check the rating count before you trust any score, including ours.

What we won't do later

Every one of these started as a small, reasonable-sounding decision somewhere else.

  • No content wall, ever. Not "read three then post," not a soft one.
  • No collecting identity, however useful verification would be.
  • No paid removals, no paid reordering, no paid effect on the Founder Index.
  • No unmarked paid placement. If something is bought, it says so on its face.

If any of that changes, the transparency log is where you'd catch it first.

The rules,
in plain language.

This is a working draft, not finished legal terms. It describes how the site is actually built so a lawyer has something concrete to mark up. Have internet or media counsel review it before you launch. Nothing here is legal advice.

What this is

A public place where current and former employees describe what it was like to work at a founder-led company. Three kinds of posting: a record of something that happened, an opinion about an officer of the company, and a vent that deletes itself after 24 hours. Ratings are separate and produce the Founder Index.

What you agree to when you post

  • It happened to you, or you saw it. Not secondhand.
  • Records are statements of fact and you believe them to be true.
  • Opinions are conclusions, and your disclosures next to them are answered honestly.
  • You name companies. You may name a founder, CEO, or other officer in an opinion. You do not name anyone below that line, no coworkers, no direct reports, no individual customers.
  • No contact details for anyone, including yourself. No home addresses, phone numbers, emails, or handles.
  • Nothing you are contractually barred from disclosing, trade secrets, customer data, material under NDA.
  • You indemnify the site for what you post. If someone sues over your words, they are your words.

What we collect

No accounts, no email required, no password. Your handle is generated in your browser and is not linked to anything about you. We do not ask who you are and we do not store it.

Anonymity, stated honestly

We can't hand over what we never collected, but that is not the same as a guarantee. Courts can compel platforms to produce whatever they do hold, server logs, IP addresses, timestamps. Sites like this have been ordered to unmask posters before. If a post would be catastrophic for you if traced, weigh that before writing it, and consider talking to an employment lawyer instead.

We deliberately blur timestamps into wide buckets, use departments instead of job titles, and never publish raw post times, because those three details together identify people at small companies.

Automated screening

Everything is scanned before it publishes. Nothing sits in a queue waiting for a human by default, most posts go up instantly.

  • Blocked outright: email addresses, phone numbers, national ID numbers, street addresses, links to personal profiles, and @handles. You cannot post these; edit and resubmit.
  • Held for review: text that looks like a person's name. The scan is a heuristic and gets this wrong sometimes, so you can send it to a moderator rather than rewrite it.

Removing your own post

There are no accounts here, so there is no password to prove a post is yours. Instead, every post hands you a deletion key at the moment you publish it, shown once, stored nowhere in readable form. Keep it and you can delete that post from any device, forever, no questions asked.

Posts made on this device are also listed below automatically, so you usually won’t need the key at all. The key matters if you clear your browser or move to another machine.

If you lose the key and clear this browser, we cannot delete your post on request. Not because we’re unwilling, because we have no way to tell you from the founder asking us to remove it. That is the trade for collecting nothing about you, and it’s worth understanding before you post.

Notice and takedown

If you are the subject of a post and believe it states something false, tell us. We aim to respond within five business days. We do not remove posts for being unflattering, and we do not remove opinions for being unwelcome, but a specific, identified factual claim that you can show is false will come down, and we log every decision either way.

Copyright claims go to our registered DMCA agent rather than through this form.

Submit a notice

Identify the specific sentence you say is false and what makes it false. Notices that just say "this is defamatory" without pointing at a statement can't be acted on.

Transparency log

Every notice we receive appears here, whatever we decide. Complainants aren’t named. The company is, along with what we did and why. If we ever start quietly removing posts for paying employers, this table is where it would show up. That is the point of publishing it.

Rules of record

  1. First-hand only. What happened to you or what you witnessed.
  2. Records name companies, not coworkers. Opinions may be about the founder, CEO, or another named officer, never anyone below that line.
  3. Records state facts. Opinions state conclusions and show the reasoning under them. Pick the right one and it gets stamped accordingly.
  4. Every opinion carries its poster's disclosures. Answer them straight; a shaded disclosure is what turns a fair opinion into a liability.
  5. Nothing you signed away. Trade secrets, customer data, and material under NDA stay out.
  6. Three flags hides a post from the ledger and sends it to an administrator for review.

Before you file

Entries are anonymous to readers, but anonymity is not a shield. Statements of fact about a named company can be litigated if they are false. Post what you can substantiate.

If you are describing illegal conduct such as wage theft, retaliation, or securities fraud, a public post is not your remedy. Talk to an employment lawyer first.
